# Exercise 04 — AI Ethics

## Objectives

- Understand ethical concerns with Generative AI
- Learn about bias, hallucinations, and responsible AI
- Know best practices for using AI responsibly

## Key Ethical Concerns

### 1. Bias
AI models learn from internet data, which contains human biases. This can lead to biased outputs.

- **Example:** A hiring AI trained on historical data might favor certain demographics.
- **Solution:** Use diverse training data, audit outputs regularly.

### 2. Hallucination
LLMs sometimes generate false information that sounds convincing.

- **Example:** AI cites a research paper that doesn't exist.
- **Solution:** Always verify AI-generated facts against reliable sources.

### 3. Privacy
AI models may expose private information from training data.

- **Example:** An AI generates an email address of a real person.
- **Solution:** Don't share sensitive data with AI tools. Use enterprise-grade solutions with privacy guarantees.

### 4. Intellectual Property
Content generated by AI raises questions about ownership and copyright.

- **Question:** Who owns AI-generated code — the user, the AI provider, or the public?
- **Solution:** Check your organization's AI policy. Some companies prohibit using AI-generated code.

### 5. Job Displacement
AI automation may change or replace certain roles.

- **Perspective:** AI is more likely to augment jobs than replace them entirely.
- **Action:** Learn to use AI as a tool to boost your productivity.

## Responsible AI Principles (Microsoft)

1. **Fairness** — AI should treat all people fairly.
2. **Reliability & Safety** — AI should operate reliably and safely.
3. **Privacy & Security** — AI should protect user data.
4. **Inclusiveness** — AI should empower everyone.
5. **Transparency** — Users should know when they're interacting with AI.
6. **Accountability** — Humans should be responsible for AI outcomes.
